How to Snake a Clogged Toilet: What Lompoc, CA Homes Need to Know

A closet auger clears most toilet clogs in minutes without scratching porcelain. Here is the method, and the signs that mean the problem is bigger.

By the Lompoc Express Plumbing teamPublished November 12, 2026Updated November 12, 2026

Use a closet auger, also called a toilet snake, not a regular drain snake. It has a rubber sleeve that protects the bowl. Feed the cable into the drain, crank the handle to break up or hook the clog and pull it back out. Most toilet clogs clear in two or three passes.

If the toilet clogs again right away, if other drains are slow or if water is rising in a tub, the clog is not in the toilet. Stop adding water and call (805) 249-7135.

Try a plunger first, the right way

A flange plunger, with the extra rubber fold on the bottom, seals better in a toilet than a flat cup. Get the bowl to about half full, seat the plunger over the drain, push slowly once to expel air and then pull up sharply. Repeat for about twenty seconds.

If the water drains, flush gently. If it does not move, move on to the auger. Never use more than one drain cleaner, and never put chemicals in a stopped toilet, because they sit in the bowl and can splash when you work on it.

How to snake the toilet

  1. Gather supplies

    Closet auger, rubber gloves, old towels and a bucket.

  2. Position the auger

    Pull the cable back into the sleeve and set the curved end into the drain opening.

  3. Feed and crank

    Push the cable in, turn the handle clockwise and push until you feel resistance.

  4. Work the clog

    Keep turning while pushing gently. The cable either breaks the clog up or hooks it.

  5. Retract and rinse

    Pull the cable back slowly while turning, then rinse it in the bowl.

  6. Test

    Flush once with the lid up. If the water rises, stop and shut the valve behind the toilet.

If you hear a hollow sound when the cable meets the clog, it is often a child's toy or a wad of wipes. If the cable stops at a solid obstruction, do not force it, because that can damage porcelain.

Hard water can contribute to the second-to-last row, since mineral scale narrows the rim jets over time. Descale them with vinegar, and see our toilet repair page if the flush stays weak.

When snaking is not enough

Call a plumber if the auger will not pass, the toilet rocks, water appears around the base or you suspect a foreign object. Pulling a toilet to clear the trap from below is a two-person job with a new wax ring when it goes back.

If other fixtures are involved, the stoppage is in the branch or main line, and the right tool is a drum cable or jetter. Read about drain cleaning in that case. Stop using water if sewage is rising.

Lompoc local note: your lateral and your responsibility

According to the City of Lompoc wastewater FAQ, lateral sewer clogs, cleanouts and maintenance are the property owner's responsibility. The lateral is the pipe from your home to the city main. A toilet clog that returns after a clean auger pass is a clue that the lateral may be partly blocked.

The city also notes that roots are a common cause of overflows and that sanitary sewer overflows must be reported. If you see sewage coming up in a yard cleanout, stop using water and call. Do not open a full cleanout.

A cheap plastic hand auger is fine for a toilet. A long power auger belongs to the plumber. Using one on porcelain can crack the bowl, and the repair costs far more than the service call.

Cleanup and sanitizing

Toilet water carries bacteria, so treat the area as dirty. Wear gloves, lay towels on the floor and wash the auger with hot soapy water afterward. Rinse the sleeve and let it dry before storing.

Wipe the floor and the base of the toilet with a disinfectant. If water splashed on a wall or cabinet, clean that too. Wash your hands for a full twenty seconds when you finish. Replace any towel that took a lot of toilet water instead of reusing it.

If the flush is still weak after a clear pass, check the water level in the tank and the rim jets. Both can slow a flush even when the drain is open.

What to tell the plumber on the phone

A short, clear description speeds up the visit. Say whether the toilet is the only fixture affected, whether it has happened before and whether anything unusual was flushed. Mention water on the floor or sewage odors.

If you tried a plunger or an auger, say what you felt. A soft resistance suggests paper. A hard stop suggests an object. A cable that spins freely and finds nothing suggests the clog is farther down.

Having this information lets the plumber bring the right tools, and it helps us give a clear price before work starts.

Prevent the next clog

  • Flush only toilet paper and human waste. Trash bin everything else.
  • Use less paper per flush, or flush twice for a heavy load.
  • Do not flush wipes, even those labeled flushable.
  • Descale rim jets with vinegar every few months in hard water.
  • Keep a flange plunger and closet auger under the sink.
