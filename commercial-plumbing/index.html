Restaurants, grease and the City of Lompoc rules

The City of Lompoc wastewater FAQ says new food service establishments must install a grease trap or interceptor that meets the most recent California Plumbing Code, and that all food service establishments will eventually be required to have one. It sets the local oil and grease discharge limit at 100 mg/L as an instantaneous maximum, subject to change.

The practical meaning is that a restaurant kitchen should know the size and location of its trap or interceptor and keep a pumping schedule. Skipped service is the common cause of overnight backups. The city also warns that some deodorizers emulsify grease and move the blockage downstream, so contact the wastewater division before using one. It offers best management practice training for restaurants, wineries and automotive businesses.

Commercial leak detection, honestly

Leak detection in a commercial building is harder than in a house. Several tenants, shared risers and long lines under slabs make a mystery meter reading hard to trace. We start with pressure isolation by zone and then use acoustic and thermal methods, just as on our leak detection page.

Be ready for a measured answer. Some commercial leaks are the landlord's, some the tenant's, and a lease decides which. We tell you where the leak is, and the responsible party fixes it.

Water heaters, fixtures and planned work

Restaurants and salons have heavy hot water demand, and a tank sized for a home will not keep up. Lompoc water is hard, with average hardness near 293 ppm in the city's 2025 report, so commercial heaters and dishwashers scale quickly without a softener or regular flushing. See water softener and filtration if scale is shortening equipment life.

For fixture swaps, tenant improvements and new lines, planned jobs are covered under plumbing installation. Permits matter on commercial work. The Lompoc building division says a permit is required before work on plumbing systems, so confirm requirements with the counter.
