Signs of a hidden leak, in the order people notice them

Meter still moving

Close every tap and appliance. If the meter dial or low-flow indicator keeps turning, water is escaping.

High water bill

A bill well above your normal month with no change in habits is a classic leak sign.

Wet ceiling or wall

Brown stains, bubbling paint or soft drywall mean water has been traveling inside the wall.

Warm floor or spot

A warm patch on a slab can mean a hot water line leaking underneath.

Low pressure

A sudden drop with no city outage can point to a leak on the supply side.

Musty smell

A damp, closed-in smell with no visible source is worth checking.

Many people start the call with a symptom, not the word leak, and that is fine. Describe what you see and we will work out the method.

How we find the leak without tearing things open

Detection starts with isolation. We shut off sections of the system to see which segment holds pressure and which loses it. That narrows a house-wide mystery to a single wall, slab or yard run.

From there, acoustic listening equipment hears the hiss of pressurized water, thermal imaging shows cool or warm patterns behind drywall and moisture meters confirm what the camera suggests. For drain lines, a sewer camera confirms cracked pipe. When the source turns out to be a slab, slab leak repair is the next step.

Meter test: the five-minute check you can do first

Most homes have a water meter in a box near the curb or property line. Turn off every fixture, ice maker and irrigation timer. Read the meter, wait fifteen minutes, then read it again without using water. Any change means a leak somewhere past the meter.

The City of Lompoc's water division takes reports of suspected leaks on the city side of the meter. Anything after the meter is the owner's side, which is where our work begins. Yard and meter-box leaks, such as a failed fitting beside the box, are a common find on that stretch.

Ceiling, wall, crawlspace and buried-line leaks

Where the leak lives changes how we approach it. A ceiling leak under a bathroom is often a drain or shower pan. A wall leak may be a supply line behind a fixture. Under-house leaks in crawlspaces show up as damp insulation, rusty straps or sagging subfloor.

Buried supply lines between the meter and the house can leak for months before the soil surfaces. Irrigation lines are a separate system and a frequent source of high bills in yards and flower beds. If pool or spa plumbing is involved, tell us on the call so we send the right tools.

Once the leak is found, repair options range from a single fitting to a section of pipe. If the pipe is failing everywhere, repiping is the lasting answer.

Lompoc water and why pinhole leaks happen

The city's 2025 water quality report shows a pH near 8.4 and average hardness near 293 ppm. Hard water leaves scale that can hide small leaks until a fitting fails, and older copper and galvanized lines still develop pinholes over time. Mineral crust around a joint is often the first visible clue.

We note that the city's lead and copper testing in 2025 found no sites over the action levels. That speaks to the water supplied to the home, not the condition of your own interior pipe.

What to do while you wait for a leak detection visit

  • Close the main valve if water is visibly spreading, then note the meter reading.
  • Do not cut into walls or floors yourself, because the wet spot is often not the source.
  • Turn off power to any wet outlet only if the panel is dry and easy to reach.
  • Photograph stains, warm floor spots and meter readings with the time.
  • Keep a record of recent water bills to show the plumber.
