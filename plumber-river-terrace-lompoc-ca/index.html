Check your builder warranty first

New homes usually come with a builder or manufacturer warranty. Before paying for a repair, check what your paperwork covers and who to call. A plumber can still help with anything outside the warranty, and can document a problem so you have a clear record to share.

That also applies to the water heater, which often has its own manufacturer terms. Keep the model and serial label visible when you call. It speeds up a water heater repair visit.

Hard water arrives in new homes too

Lompoc draws its drinking water from groundwater wells. The city's 2025 water quality report lists average hardness of 293 ppm, which counts as hard. A new house does not change that. Scale can build up in a tank or tankless heater and on faucet aerators from the first year.

A softener or filter choice is easiest to make while the house is new, because the plumber can place it where the lines already run. A yearly flush also helps any tankless water heater keep its flow.

Adding fixtures and finishing a unit

Families often add a laundry sink, an outdoor spigot or a bathroom as the home settles in. Plumbing installation is cleaner when it is planned before drywall or flooring goes in. If your plan includes an accessory unit, ask about water and drain capacity before you design the room.

For everyday fixes, a faucet repair is usually a cartridge or seal, not a full replacement.
