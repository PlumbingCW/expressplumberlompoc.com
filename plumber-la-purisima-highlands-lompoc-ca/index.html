Larger homes, bigger hot water demand

A home with several bedrooms and baths asks a lot of its water heater. Morning showers, laundry and the dishwasher can all run at once. If hot water fades halfway through a busy morning, the heater may be too small for current use, or sediment may have cut its capacity.

Two answers are common. A larger tank fixes capacity. A tankless water heater supplies hot water on demand, though it needs the right gas and venting. A plumber can size either one to your home.

Protect fixtures and appliances from scale

The city's 2025 report puts average hardness at 293 ppm, so scale gathers on shower heads, faucets and inside heaters. A softener or filter reduces that, and in a home with many fixtures the benefit adds up.

Ask a plumber what suits your family. Some households want softer water, and others only want filtered drinking water.

Hidden leaks in a larger home

In a bigger house, a small leak can run for a long time before it shows. Watch the water bill and listen for running water with everything off. A leak detection visit finds the source without tearing out finished walls.

If you are adding a bath or an outdoor kitchen, plan plumbing installation early, so lines and drains fit the design.
