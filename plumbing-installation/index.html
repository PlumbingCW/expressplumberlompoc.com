Permits and inspections in Lompoc

The City of Lompoc building division says you must apply for a permit before you work on plumbing, gas, mechanical or electrical systems. It also states that any addition or relocation of water, sewer, gas or electrical lines always needs a permit. Fixing leaks or clearing stoppages is exempt only if you are not changing the layout.

That has a practical meaning for you. A bathroom move needs a permit even if the walls are open anyway. We handle permit paperwork and inspections where they apply, and the building counter can answer specific questions. Permits can be applied for online, and the office is open on weekdays.

California adopts the plumbing code in Title 24, Part 5, and the 2025 edition has an effective date of January 1, 2026.

Gas line work: safety comes first

If you smell gas, do not call us first. Leave the building, do not touch switches, and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. We do not promise a response time for suspected gas leaks, because the utility is the first responder.

For planned gas work, such as a new range or dryer line, we pressure test every connection and check appliance clearances. Gas line installation and inspection are among the services callers ask about, and they require a permit and inspection in most cases.

Remodel plumbing: plan the order

  1. Layout and rough-in

    Drains and supply lines placed before walls close.

  2. Inspection

    Rough plumbing inspected where required.

  3. Close and finish

    Walls, tile and cabinets installed over inspected lines.

  4. Set fixtures

    Toilets, sinks, faucets and valves installed and tested.

Calling us before demolition saves money, because moving a drain after the slab or tile is down costs far more. Slab homes are the toughest, and slab leak repair is the page to read if lines under the floor are already suspect.

New construction and additions

New construction plumbing is done in stages: underground drains, rough-in and finish. We coordinate with the general contractor and the inspector. Smaller additions, such as a laundry room or half bath, follow the same sequence on a smaller scale.

If your project includes a new water heater, see water heater installation. For restaurants and offices, commercial plumbing covers the extra rules.
