When a failed water heater is an emergency

Replacement becomes urgent in a few clear situations.

  • Water is leaking from the tank body, seam or base rather than from a pipe fitting.
  • The heater is rusted through at the bottom, or you see rust-colored water at every hot tap.
  • There is no hot water and the unit is past about ten years old.
  • A relief valve is discharging constantly, and the tank is bulging or rumbling.
  • The burner or elements have been replaced already and the heater fails again.

A heater that is leaking should not be left running. Water on the floor under a gas unit is also a reason to shut the gas off until it is checked.

Permits, code and earthquake strapping in Lompoc

The City of Lompoc building division says a permit is needed before you work on electrical, gas, mechanical or plumbing systems, and its page says that includes replacing systems. Clearing stoppages or fixing leaks is exempt only if you are not rearranging anything. We pull permits where they apply, and you can confirm any detail with the building counter.

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires new and replacement water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake motion, at a minimum in line with the California Plumbing Code. The 2025 edition of Title 24 carries an effective date of January 1, 2026. Every replacement we do includes proper strapping.

Choosing the new heater

Standard tank heaters remain the lowest upfront cost and fit most Lompoc garages and closets. Tankless units cost more and save floor space, and they need a gas line and venting sized for them. Heat pump hybrids use less electricity but need clearance and air.

Lompoc's water is hard, with average hardness near 293 ppm in the city's 2025 report. A tank in that water lasts longer with a yearly flush, and a tankless unit needs descaling. If tankless appeals, read the tankless water heater page first, and for a planned swap with no rush, see water heater installation.

What a replacement visit includes

  1. Shut off and drain

    Gas or power off, water off, the old tank drained safely.

  2. Disconnect and remove

    Old heater unhooked, flue and connections inspected, old unit hauled away.

  3. Set and connect

    New heater set, new flex lines, valves and a relief valve discharge line installed.

  4. Strap and test

    Seismic strapping fitted, gas or power tested, leak and venting checks done.

Most single-tank swaps are finished in one visit. Changing fuel type, moving the heater or adding venting takes longer.
