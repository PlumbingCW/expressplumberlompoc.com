How emergency dispatch works

What happens when you call the emergency plumber line

People who call an emergency plumber usually ask the same few things: can someone come tonight, what will it cost and what should I do in the meantime. Here is how the line answers each one.

  1. Call and describe it

    A real person answers at any hour. Tell them what you see, what you hear and where the water is going.

  2. Quick triage

    The dispatcher asks a few questions to decide how urgent it is and what the plumber should bring.

  3. Appointment set

    You are offered the next available plumber. Same-day appointments are offered when available.

  4. Clear price before work starts

    The plumber explains the problem and the price first. You decide whether to go ahead.

  5. Repair and cleanup of the work area

    The plumber fixes the problem, tests it and leaves the area tidy.

If the problem is not an emergency, say so. Daytime appointments for slow drains, dripping faucets and planned upgrades are easier to schedule and give the plumber more room to work.

While you wait

What to do while the emergency plumber is on the way

Shut off the main waterTurn the main valve clockwise until it stops. Most homes have it near the street side or by the water heater.
Switch off the water heaterTurn the gas to pilot or flip the breaker so the tank does not heat while empty.
Keep electricity away from waterIf water reaches outlets, appliances or the panel, leave that area and turn off the breaker only if it is dry and safe to reach.
Protect what you canMove rugs, furniture and electronics out of the wet area and photograph damage for your insurer.
Plumbing in Lompoc

What Lompoc homes ask of their plumbing

Lompoc grew from a temperance colony incorporated in 1888 into a city of about 44,000 people, and a large part of that growth came after the Air Force began expanding the area in 1958. Homes from those decades often have older supply lines and drain pipe that are now due for attention. If your house dates from the late twentieth century, a camera look at the drain and a pressure check on the supply are both reasonable.

The city draws its drinking water from groundwater wells, and its 2025 water quality report lists average hardness of 293 ppm. That is hard water, and it leaves scale in water heaters, faucets and tankless units. In practice that means a yearly heater flush and a look at water softener options pay for themselves.

The earthquake of November 4, 1927 collapsed chimneys and cracked highways in the valley. California now requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped under Health and Safety Code section 19211. If your heater stands free of the wall, ask the plumber to check the straps on any visit. A well-strapped tank is much less likely to rupture a gas or water line in a quake.

Plumbers also work to the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Ask whether your project needs a City permit, since replacing a system usually does.

Near the coast, fog and salt air are part of daily life. Exposed outdoor fittings, hose bibs and tankless heater vents corrode sooner here than inland, so stainless or coated hardware is worth the small extra cost.
